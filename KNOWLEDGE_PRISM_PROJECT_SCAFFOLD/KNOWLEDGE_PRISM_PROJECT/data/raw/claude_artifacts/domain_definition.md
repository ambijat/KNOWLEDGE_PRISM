## The domain, restated

The domain is a **two-layer structure**, and your stated research goal lives at their intersection:

**Layer A — Empirical / substantive (the "what"):** the geopolitics of a connected Eurasian security space — Afghanistan/Taliban core, extended through South Asia/Pakistan, Central Asia/Eurasia, Chinese connectivity (BRI/OBOR/CPEC), and Russian energy corridors.

**Layer B — Theoretical & methodological (the "how we know"):** an IR-theory canon (realism/neorealism, English School, constructivism, critical theory) resting on a **knowledge-structures toolkit** — systems theory, semiotics, grounded theory, critical/transcendental realism, social-network analysis, game theory, computational/agent-based simulation, and AI-for-IR.

Of the 392 books analyzed from the bridge folders, the orientation split is **theory 220 · mixed 70 · empirical 57 · method 45** — i.e., the deep-read core is predominantly conceptual, exactly the material a knowledge-structures project draws on.

## The conceptual vocabulary, from actual book content

**IR-theory canon** (most frequent concepts across the corpus): international relations theory, balance of power, constructivism, realism/neorealism, global governance, international society, sovereignty, international political economy, English School theory, critical theory, unipolarity, hegemony, democratic peace, liberal internationalism.

**Knowledge-structures / generic-induction seam** (concepts from the method & epistemology folders — the material for your research question): Peircean semiotics and sign systems, cartosemiotics/visual semiotics, grounded theory (theoretical sampling, qualitative coding), transcendental & critical realism, immanent critique, general systems theory, game theory, natural language processing, phenomenology, methodological pluralism, social simulation, agent-based/complex adaptive systems.

That second list is the heart of it. Your library already contains, in physical form, the toolkit for asking *how scientific models with generic induction can represent knowledge structures in IR* — semiotics (how signs carry meaning), grounded theory (how categories are induced from data), systems theory (how to formalize wholes), and computational/AI methods (how to model and simulate). These sit right next to the IR theory they would be applied to.

## The one-sentence domain statement (v2)

> **The geopolitics of a connected Eurasian security space (Afghanistan/Taliban core; South/Central Asia, Chinese connectivity, Russian energy periphery), studied through an IR-theory canon and an interpretive-and-formal methods toolkit — semiotics, grounded theory, systems theory, network analysis, and computational/AI modeling — whose intersection is the question of how formal, inductive models can represent the knowledge structures through which international relations are understood.**

## Where this points for Step 2 (pulling papers)

The two-layer structure sharpens the retrieval targets. Highest value is the **intersection of Layer A and Layer B** — where your empirical geopolitics meets a knowledge-representation method:

1. **Formal/network models of regional security complexes** — RSCT (Buzan-Wæver) rendered as graphs or systems, not prose.
2. **Computational & network approaches to IR knowledge** — semantic/discourse networks, topic models, agent-based simulation of geopolitics (connects your `socialnetwork_analysis` and `twittergeopolitics` material to the empirical core).
3. **Semiotics, grounded theory, and systems theory *applied to* IR** — the applied literature that turns your method shelf onto your empirical domain (the `AI_InternationalRelationstheory` folder is the seed).
4. **Ontology & knowledge representation in the social sciences** — how induction and formal models transfer into interpretive domains; directly answers your stated question.
5. **Post-2021 empirical refresh** on the Taliban/Afghanistan core and BRI/energy corridors, where both Zotero and the books thin out after 2020.
